#  MGB Open Access License 1.0 Best Practice Guide

Historically the findings of this academic research were published in scholarly journals that restricted access to those who are granted permission via their university affiliation, or by purchasing access to individual articles.

This historical system for producing and distributing publicly funded research articles was expensive and did not take advantage of the innovative possibilities of many digital innovations, such as open licensing. Without a free-flowing system, access to the results of scientific research is limited to institutions that are able to commit to journal subscriptions, which does not allow for broad redistribution or data mining without additional permissions from the rightsholder. This closed system limits the impact on the scientific and scholarly community and progress is slowed significantly.

Open access publishing was created as a direct response to these closed media practices. Open access literature is defined as "digital, online, free of charge, and free of most copyright and licensing restrictions." The recommendations of the Budapest Open Access Declaration — including the use of liberal licensing (such as via Creative Commons and this MGBOA 1.0 License) — are widely recognized in the research community as the standard in making a work truly Open Access.

The MGB Open Access 1.0 License allows creators and researchers to retain a specific set of rights in accordance with their academic and commercial preferences, while still making their work available for widespread consumption.

# Use Cases
Open access initiatives support the right to share information, including academic and scholarly research, freely and for the benefit of the public, without concern for licensing fees. Instead of hiding information behind paywalls and requiring institutional access, open access publishers make information accessible to anyone. These publishers require specialized licenses, such as Creative Commons licenses and MGBOA 1.0, in order to publish works.

# Foundational Summary
Traditional copyright operates on the principle of "All rights reserved." Creative Commons and other Open Access licenses are not an alternative to copyright, but rather a modification of traditional copyright. These licenses, including MGBOA 1.0, allow a creator to reserve some of their rights as creator, while waiving other rights, such as the exclusive right to share their work, as they see fit.

Although there are six active Creative Commons licenses currently in practice, where Licensees can do (nearly) anything they want with the code, MGBOA 1.0 not only adds a number of use restrictions, including a Research – Non-Commercial use restriction, it expands the scope of the licensed work to possibly include software, code, data, and — as of this revision — artificial intelligence and machine learning Models, including model weights and related artifacts. (Note: Creative Commons recommends free and open source software licenses for software, rather than its own licenses.)

# Requirements
Anyone who uses Open Access materials licensed under MGBOA 1.0 is allowed to use and distribute the Licensed Materials solely for non-commercial, non-revenue generating, research purposes (open access publishing being a core aspect of modern research).

Unlike the MGB Open Source License 1.0, if a Licensee chooses to redistribute the journal article, data, Model, or other licensed materials with any modifications, the Licensee must deliver a copy of the modifications (rather than merely the identification of changes required under MGB Open Source License 1.0) to the Licensor and the Journal or repository as indicated. This follows the most recent Open Journal guidance regarding the expectation that the published article in its final form (the Version of Record, or "VoR") can be relied upon as accurate, complete, and citable. Discussions, reports, and requests that a change be made to a published article accordingly happen in concert between the Licensee, Licensor, and the Journal, and commonly occur regarding Name Changes, Errors or Omissions Impacting Indexability, Errors or Concerns Related to Validity or Integrity, and Corrections to Supplemental Materials and Research Data.

# A Two-Tier Redistribution Structure
The License distinguishes between what Licensor may do with a delivered modification and what other recipients (i.e., downstream Licensees who obtain the modification from the designated repository) may do with it:

Licensor's rights. Once a Licensee delivers a modification under the sharing-back obligation, Licensor receives a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free license — including the right to sublicense through multiple tiers — to use, reproduce, modify, publicly display, publicly perform, and distribute that contribution, and to incorporate it into the Data or Materials on any terms Licensor deems appropriate, including terms that differ from this License. This allows Licensor (and the Journal or repository, where applicable) to operate, curate, and redistribute the combined Open Access work, including into subsequent commercial or non-commercial editions, without being bound by the same non-commercial ceiling that applies to ordinary Licensees.

Other recipients' rights. Any Licensee who obtains a delivered modification from the designated repository continues to receive it strictly under the same non-commercial, non-revenue-generating, research-purpose terms as the rest of this License, with no right to further sublicense. The non-commercial restriction that defines MGBOA 1.0 is therefore preserved for the research community at large — it is only Licensor's own administrative and distribution rights that are broadened.
Contributor's own rights are unaffected. Delivering a modification to Licensor under the sharing-back obligation does not surrender or narrow a contributor's own right to continue using, modifying, or distributing that modification independently, subject to the remaining terms of this License.

# Contributor Assurances
Because Licensor's rights to redistribute contributed modifications are now broader, a Licensee who delivers a modification represents that: (i) they have the legal right and authority to grant the license described above; (ii) to the best of their knowledge, the modification does not include patient data or other personal data unless properly de-identified in accordance with applicable law, including HIPAA where applicable; and (iii) they have preserved all applicable attributions, copyright notices, and third-party license terms within the modification. This is a lighter-weight assurance than a formal Contribution Agreement, but it gives Licensor a baseline representation to rely on when incorporating community-contributed corrections, data, or Model updates into the Version of Record or a maintained repository.

Licensees are responsible for obtaining and maintaining their own licenses, at their own cost and expense, to any such third-party rights. Patent Non-Assertion (New). While MGBOA 1.0 continues to grant no affirmative patent license, the License now includes a mutual covenant among Licensor, Licensees, and Contributors not to assert patent infringement claims against one another arising from a party's Use of the Data or Materials as permitted under the License, limited to patent claims necessarily infringed by the Data or Materials in the form made available. This covenant is a promise not to sue, not a transfer of patent rights, and does not extend to any use outside the scope of the License. The covenant terminates automatically as to any party who first brings a qualifying patent claim against another party under the License, so a party who initiates patent litigation over the Materials loses the benefit of the covenant as against the party it sued. Researchers and institutions should understand this as protection against patent surprises within the community of Users, not as a general patent license, and should continue to route any separate technology-transfer or licensing questions through their own institution's process.
